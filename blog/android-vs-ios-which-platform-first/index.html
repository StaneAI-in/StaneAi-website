Android vs iOS: which platform should you build your first app for?

By the StaneAI team · 19 November 2026

One of the first questions in any mobile app project is: should we build for Android, iOS, or both? There is no universal answer, but there is a clear way to decide. It comes down to who your users are, how the app will make money, and how much time and budget you have for the first version.

1. Start with where your users are

Android has the larger share of smartphones worldwide, and in India it is used on the large majority of phones. If your app targets a broad audience in India — students, shop owners, everyday consumers — Android is usually the natural starting point.

iPhone usage is much higher in markets like the United States, and among some professional and higher-income audiences everywhere. If your first customers are in the US, or your audience skews that way, iOS deserves serious consideration.

Practical tip: don’t guess. Ask ten target users which phone they use, or look at your existing website analytics for the device split.

2. Consider how the app will make money

App-store industry reports have consistently shown that iOS users, on average, spend more on apps and in-app purchases than Android users. If your plan relies on paid subscriptions or purchases, that matters. If your app is free, ad-supported, or supports an existing business (for example, ordering or bookings), reach usually matters more than spend per user — which often favours Android in India.

3. Look at practical differences

  • Developer accounts: at the time of writing, Google Play charges a one-time registration fee, while the Apple Developer Program has an annual fee. Check both stores for current pricing.
  • Review process: both stores review apps before they go live. Apple’s review is known to be stricter on design and policy guidelines, so plan time for possible feedback.
  • Device variety: Android runs on many manufacturers and screen sizes, so testing needs to cover more devices. iOS has fewer device models to support.
  • Development tools: building iOS apps natively requires a Mac and Xcode; Android development works on Windows, macOS or Linux.

4. The third option: cross-platform

Frameworks like Flutter and React Native let one codebase run on both Android and iOS. For many apps — content, e-commerce, booking, productivity, internal tools — this is a smart way to launch on both platforms without doubling the work.

Native development (Kotlin for Android, Swift for iOS) still makes sense when the app depends heavily on advanced device features, complex animations or maximum performance, or when you only need one platform.

A simple way to decide

  • Audience mainly in India, free or ad-supported app: start with Android, or go cross-platform.
  • Audience mainly in the US, subscription-based app: start with iOS, or go cross-platform.
  • Audience on both and a limited budget: cross-platform is usually the best value.
  • Heavy hardware or performance needs: native, starting with the platform your core users prefer.

Validate first, then expand

Whatever you choose, the first version’s job is to prove that people want the app. Launch a focused version, measure how people actually use it, and then expand to the second platform with confidence — and with much better knowledge of what to build.
