How to publish your app on Google Play and the App Store

By the StaneAI team · 27 November 2026

Your app is built and tested. Now it has to get through two very different gatekeepers: Google Play and Apple’s App Store. Both have their own accounts, fees, forms and review rules, and small mistakes here can delay a launch by days or weeks. This is the checklist we follow when we take an app live for a client.

Key takeaways

  • Create developer accounts early — verification can take days, longer for company accounts.
  • Google Play: a one-time registration fee; new personal accounts must run a closed test with real testers before going public.
  • Apple: an annual Apple Developer Program fee; every release goes through App Review.
  • Both stores need a privacy policy, honest data-use disclosures and, if users can sign up, a way to delete their account.
  • Publish under your own company account, not your developer’s, so you always own the app.

Store rules and fees change from time to time. The steps below are accurate at the time of writing; always check the current Google Play Console and App Store Connect help pages before you submit.

Before you start

  • Ownership. The app should be published from an account in your company’s name. If an agency or freelancer publishes it from their account, transferring it later is slow and sometimes messy. Give your developer access to your account instead.
  • Legal basics. A public privacy policy URL, terms of use if needed, and a support email or page.
  • A real, finished product. Placeholder screens, “coming soon” buttons and broken links are among the fastest ways to get rejected.

Step 1: Developer accounts and fees

Google Play Console

  • One-time registration fee (US$25 at the time of writing).
  • Choose a personal or organisation account. Businesses should use an organisation account; it needs company details and a D-U-N-S number, and it skips some testing requirements that apply to new personal accounts.
  • Identity verification is required and can take a few days.

Apple Developer Program

  • Annual membership fee (US$99 per year at the time of writing; local pricing may apply).
  • Enrol as an organisation to show your company name as the seller. This also needs a D-U-N-S number and a legal entity — a private limited company or LLP works.
  • Enrolment can take from a day to a couple of weeks, so start it while the app is still in development.

Getting a D-U-N-S number is free but can take time. Apply as soon as you know you will publish.

Step 2: Prepare the build

  • Android: an Android App Bundle (.aab), signed for release, targeting the recent Android API level Google currently requires. Enrol in Play App Signing so Google keeps your signing key safe.
  • iOS: an archive built with a current version of Xcode and uploaded to App Store Connect, with the correct bundle ID, certificates and provisioning profiles.
  • Version numbers: every upload needs a higher build number than the last one.
  • Test on real devices — including an older, cheaper Android phone and slow networks, which is how many users in India will open your app.
  • Crash reporting and analytics switched on, so you can see problems after launch.

Step 3: Store listing

The listing is your app’s shop window, and it also affects how the app is found in store search.

  • Name and subtitle / short description — clear words people actually search for, not just your brand.
  • Full description — what the app does and who it is for, in plain language. Do not stuff keywords or mention other brands.
  • Screenshots for the required phone (and tablet, if supported) sizes, showing real screens.
  • App icon, feature graphic (Google Play) and optional preview video.
  • Category, contact email and privacy policy URL.
  • Age / content rating questionnaire on both stores.

Step 4: Privacy and policy forms

  • Google Play Data safety form and Apple privacy details (“nutrition labels”): declare what data you collect, why, and whether it is shared — including data collected by SDKs such as analytics, ads or crash reporting.
  • Account deletion: if users can create an account, both stores expect a way to delete it from inside the app, and Google also asks for a web link.
  • Permissions: only request what you use, and explain why. Sensitive permissions like background location, SMS or call logs need strong justification.
  • Login for reviewers: if the app needs an account or OTP, give reviewers a working demo login and instructions.
  • In India, also check your obligations under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act — consent, purpose and data retention.

Step 5: Testing and release on Google Play

  1. Internal testing — share with your own team in minutes.
  2. Closed testing — new personal developer accounts must run a closed test with a minimum number of opted-in testers for a set period (at the time of writing, 12 testers for 14 continuous days) before they can apply for production access. Plan for this in your timeline.
  3. Production — submit for review. Reviews often finish within a few days, but first submissions can take longer.
  4. Staged rollout — release to a small percentage of users first, watch for crashes, then go to 100%.

Step 6: TestFlight and App Review on iOS

  1. TestFlight — upload the build and test with your team; external testers need a short beta review.
  2. Submit for App Review — with screenshots, privacy details, review notes and a demo login.
  3. Review — most apps are reviewed within a day or two, but rejections and re-submissions can add days.
  4. Release — manually, automatically after approval, or as a phased release over seven days.

Common reasons apps get rejected

  • Crashes, broken buttons or placeholder content during review.
  • No working demo account, or OTP login that reviewers cannot complete.
  • Privacy declarations that do not match what the app or its SDKs actually collect.
  • Missing in-app account deletion.
  • On iOS, selling digital content or subscriptions without using in-app purchase where Apple requires it.
  • An app that is just a website in a wrapper, with little app-specific value (Apple is strict about this).
  • Asking for permissions the app does not clearly need.

After launch

Publishing is the start, not the finish. Watch crash reports and reviews in the first week, reply to user feedback, and plan regular updates. Both stores require apps to keep up with new OS versions and policy changes, so budget time every year for maintenance — our app maintenance cost guide, app timeline guide and cost guide include this.

Common questions

How much does it cost to publish an app on Google Play and the App Store?

At the time of writing, Google Play charges a one-time registration fee of US$25 and the Apple Developer Program costs US$99 per year. Store commissions apply only if you sell digital goods or subscriptions.

How long does app store review take?

Apple usually reviews apps within a day or two. Google Play reviews often finish within a few days, but new accounts and first submissions can take longer, and new personal Play accounts must first complete a closed test.

Should I publish the app under my account or my developer’s?

Under your own company account. Then you control the listing, reviews, data and revenue, and you can change development partners without transferring the app.

Do I need a company to publish an app?

No — individuals can publish with personal accounts. But an organisation account shows your business name as the seller, builds trust, and on Google Play avoids the closed-testing requirement for new personal accounts.

The bottom line

Start the developer accounts early, publish under your own company name, be honest in the privacy forms, give reviewers a way in, and leave time in the plan for testing and review. Do that, and the stores become a checklist rather than a roadblock.
