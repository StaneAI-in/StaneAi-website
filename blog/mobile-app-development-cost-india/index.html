How much does it cost to build a mobile app in India?

By the StaneAI team · 21 November 2026

“How much will my app cost?” is usually the first question founders and business owners ask — and the honest answer is “it depends”. That answer is only useful if you know what it depends on. This guide explains the main cost drivers for mobile app development in India, gives broad indicative ranges, and shows practical ways to spend less without ending up with an app nobody wants to use.

Why app quotes vary so much

Two agencies can quote very different prices for what sounds like the same app. Usually that is because they have imagined different apps. One quote may include a custom admin panel, a designed interface, automated testing and three months of support; another may cover only the screens you described. Before comparing numbers, compare what is actually included.

Most of the cost of an app is people’s time: product planning, design, development, testing and project management. Anything that adds time — more features, more platforms, more integrations, more polish — adds cost.

The main cost drivers

1. Features and complexity

This is the biggest factor by far. A simple app with a handful of screens and basic forms is a very different project from one with real-time chat, payments, maps, video or offline sync. Features that typically add significant effort include:

  • User accounts with different roles (for example customers, vendors and admins)
  • Payments, subscriptions and refunds
  • Real-time features such as chat, live tracking or notifications tied to events
  • Offline mode and data syncing
  • Search, filters and recommendations
  • AI features such as chatbots, document reading or image recognition

2. Platforms: Android, iOS or both

Building two separate native apps (Kotlin for Android, Swift for iOS) roughly means building and maintaining two codebases. Cross-platform frameworks such as Flutter and React Native let one codebase run on both, which usually reduces cost for typical business apps. If you are unsure which platform to start with, see our guide on choosing between Android and iOS.

3. Design

Using well-designed standard components is faster than creating a fully custom visual style with bespoke illustrations and animations. Good design is not optional — confusing apps get uninstalled — but the level of custom polish is a choice you can make based on your audience and budget.

4. Backend and admin panel

Most apps need more than the app itself. Behind the scenes there is usually a server and database (the backend), an API the app talks to, and an admin panel where your team manages users, content and orders. Founders often forget to budget for the admin panel, yet it is frequently a substantial part of the work.

5. Integrations

Connecting to payment gateways, SMS and WhatsApp providers, maps, shipping partners, accounting software or your existing systems all takes time. Well-documented services are quicker to integrate; older or poorly documented systems can take much longer than expected.

6. Testing and launch

Android runs on a wide range of devices and screen sizes, so testing needs to cover more ground. Both app stores review apps before publishing, and preparing store listings, screenshots and privacy details is part of the launch work.

7. Maintenance after launch

An app is never truly “finished”. Operating system updates, new device sizes, security fixes, server hosting, third-party API changes and user feedback all need ongoing attention. Plan a recurring budget for this from day one rather than treating launch as the end of spending.

Indicative cost ranges in India

The ranges below are broad, rough estimates to help you plan — not quotes. Actual costs vary widely with scope, team experience, location and how clearly requirements are defined. Always get a quote based on your specific requirements.

  • Simple app (a few screens, basic forms, simple backend, one platform or cross-platform): often in the range of a few lakh rupees.
  • Medium-complexity app (user accounts, payments, admin panel, notifications, a few integrations): commonly several lakh to a couple of tens of lakh rupees.
  • Complex app (real-time features, multiple user roles, marketplace logic, AI features, heavy integrations): can go well beyond that, and is usually best built in phases.

Be cautious of quotes that are dramatically lower than others for the same scope. Ask what is excluded — design, backend, admin panel, testing, store submission and post-launch support are common gaps.

How to reduce the cost without cutting corners

Start with an MVP

A minimum viable product (MVP) is the smallest version of the app that solves the core problem for your first users. Write down every feature you want, then ask of each one: “Would early users stop using the app if this was missing?” If the answer is no, move it to a later phase. Launching smaller lets you learn from real usage before spending on features people may not want.

Go cross-platform where it fits

For most content, e-commerce, booking, productivity and internal apps, a cross-platform framework lets you launch on Android and iOS together from one codebase. Native development still makes sense for apps that depend heavily on device hardware or demanding performance.

Use proven services instead of building everything

Authentication, payments, push notifications, analytics and hosting are available as reliable managed services. Using them is usually faster and safer than building your own.

Define requirements clearly

Vague requirements lead to rework, and rework is expensive. Simple user flows, rough sketches of key screens and a list of “must have” versus “nice to have” features help any development team estimate accurately and build the right thing the first time.

Plan in phases

Splitting the project into phases — MVP, then improvements based on feedback — spreads the cost over time and keeps each decision grounded in real data.

Questions to ask before you accept a quote

  • Which features, screens and platforms are included?
  • Are design, backend, admin panel and testing part of the price?
  • Who owns the source code and accounts after the project?
  • How are changes to scope handled and priced?
  • What support and maintenance is offered after launch, and at what cost?
  • How often will you see working progress?

The bottom line

The cost of building a mobile app in India depends mostly on what the app needs to do, how many platforms it runs on and how polished it must be at launch. Start with a focused MVP, choose cross-platform when it fits, budget for maintenance, and insist on a clear, itemised scope. That combination gives you the best chance of launching something useful at a cost you can plan for.
