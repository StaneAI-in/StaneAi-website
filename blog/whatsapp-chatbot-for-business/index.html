WhatsApp chatbot for business: a practical guide for Indian companies

By the StaneAI team · 27 November 2026

In India, your customers are already on WhatsApp. They message to ask about prices, check an order, book an appointment or complain when something goes wrong. For a small team, answering every message by hand quickly becomes a full-time job. A WhatsApp chatbot can take care of the repetitive questions instantly, at any hour, and pass the rest to a person. This guide explains how it works, what it costs to plan for and how to launch one without annoying your customers.

The short answer

A WhatsApp chatbot is software connected to your business WhatsApp number through Meta’s official WhatsApp Business Platform. It reads incoming messages and replies automatically — with fixed menus, with AI that understands free-text questions, or with a mix of both. A good one answers common questions, collects details, updates customers on orders or bookings, and hands the conversation to your team when it should.

What a WhatsApp chatbot can do

  • Answer common questions — prices, timings, locations, policies, product details.
  • Take bookings and enquiries — collect name, requirement and preferred time, then confirm.
  • Share order and delivery status — by looking up the order in your system.
  • Qualify leads — ask a few questions and send serious enquiries to your sales team with the details already filled in.
  • Send updates customers asked for — confirmations, reminders and receipts.
  • Hand over to a human — when the question is unusual, sensitive or the customer simply asks for a person.

WhatsApp Business app vs the WhatsApp Business Platform

These are easy to confuse:

  • WhatsApp Business app — the free app on a phone. It has quick replies, greeting and away messages, labels and a catalogue. It is fine for a shop owner replying personally, but it is not built for a real chatbot or for several team members.
  • WhatsApp Business Platform (often called the WhatsApp API) — Meta’s official platform for businesses to connect WhatsApp to software. Chatbots, CRM integrations and shared team inboxes are built on this. You access it directly through Meta’s Cloud API or through an approved Business Solution Provider.

Avoid unofficial tools that automate a normal WhatsApp account. They break WhatsApp’s terms and can get your number banned — a serious risk when that number is how customers reach you.

Rule-based, AI or both?

Rule-based (menu) bots

The customer picks from buttons or lists: “1. Book appointment 2. Track order 3. Talk to us”. These are predictable, cheap to run and good for simple, structured tasks. They struggle when people type questions in their own words.

AI chatbots

An AI chatbot understands free-text messages — including mixed Hindi and English — and answers from your own information such as FAQs, price lists and policies. It feels far more natural, but it must be set up carefully so it answers only from approved information and admits when it does not know. Our guide to planning an AI chatbot that actually helps customers covers this in detail.

The practical choice

Many good bots combine both: buttons for the common, structured paths (booking, order status) and AI for everything people type freely, with a clear “talk to a person” option at all times.

Rules to know before you start

  • Customers must opt in. You can only message people who have agreed to hear from you on WhatsApp.
  • The 24-hour window. When a customer messages you, your business can reply freely for a limited time. Outside that window, you can only start a conversation with a pre-approved message template.
  • Templates need approval. Messages such as reminders, order updates or offers are written as templates and approved by Meta before you can send them.
  • Business verification. To unlock higher messaging limits and features, Meta asks you to verify your business.

Meta updates these rules and its pricing from time to time, so check the current WhatsApp Business Platform documentation when you plan.

What it costs to plan for

The total cost has three parts:

  1. WhatsApp messaging charges — Meta charges for certain types of messages, mainly the template messages your business sends. Replies to customers inside the service window are usually much cheaper or free. Rates vary by country and message category.
  2. Platform or provider fees — if you use a Business Solution Provider, they may charge a monthly fee for their tools and inbox.
  3. Building the bot — designing the conversations, connecting your systems (orders, bookings, CRM), adding AI and testing. This is a one-time project cost, plus small ongoing improvements.

If you use AI, there is also a usage cost for the AI model, which grows with the number of conversations. For most small businesses it is modest compared with the time saved.

How to launch a WhatsApp chatbot, step by step

  1. List your top questions. Look at the last few weeks of WhatsApp chats and calls. The ten most common questions are your starting point.
  2. Pick one or two jobs for version one. For example: answer FAQs and take bookings. Keep it focused — the same MVP thinking that works for apps works for chatbots.
  3. Get a number and access. Use a dedicated business number on the WhatsApp Business Platform, through Meta directly or a provider.
  4. Write the conversations. Short messages, clear buttons, a friendly tone and an easy way to reach a person.
  5. Connect your data. Price lists, FAQs, order or booking systems — whatever the bot needs to give correct answers.
  6. Test with real people. Ask staff and a few loyal customers to try it and break it before launch.
  7. Launch and review weekly. Read conversations the bot could not handle, then improve answers and add the next most common questions.

Common mistakes

  • No way to reach a human. Nothing frustrates customers faster.
  • Long menus. If people must tap through five levels to get an answer, they will give up.
  • Spamming offers. Too many promotional messages lead to blocks and reports, which can limit what your number is allowed to send.
  • AI with no guardrails. An AI bot that guesses prices or policies does more harm than good. It should answer from approved information and say when it is not sure.
  • Set and forget. Prices and policies change. Someone should own the bot and keep it up to date.

Common questions

Can I use my existing WhatsApp number?

Often yes, but a number registered on the WhatsApp Business Platform is generally used through software rather than as a normal phone app. Many businesses use a separate dedicated number for the bot to keep things simple.

Can the chatbot reply in Hindi?

Yes. Menu bots can be written in any language, and AI chatbots can understand and reply in Hindi, English and mixed “Hinglish” messages.

Will a chatbot replace my support team?

No — it removes the repetitive part of their work. Your team spends its time on the conversations that really need a person.

How long does it take to set up?

A focused bot that answers FAQs and takes simple bookings can often be live in a few weeks. Bots that connect to several systems or need custom AI take longer.

The bottom line

A WhatsApp chatbot meets Indian customers where they already are. Use the official WhatsApp Business Platform, start with one or two jobs, always offer a way to reach a person, and improve the bot every week from real conversations. Done that way, it saves your team hours and gives customers faster answers.
