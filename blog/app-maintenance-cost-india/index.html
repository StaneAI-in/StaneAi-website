App maintenance cost in India: what to budget after launch

By the StaneAI team · 28 November 2026

Most founders plan carefully for the cost of building an app — and then get surprised by what it costs to keep it running. Phones get new operating systems every year, app stores change their rules, servers need watching and users report bugs. This guide explains what app maintenance actually covers, how to estimate it in India, and how to keep it under control.

Key takeaways

  • Maintenance is not optional: apps that are not updated break on new phones and can be removed from the stores.
  • Split your budget into two parts — the team’s work (fixes, updates, small changes) and running costs (hosting, SMS, maps, AI APIs).
  • A common planning rule of thumb is 15–25% of the build cost per year for the team’s work; running costs come on top and grow with users.
  • Clean code, automated tests and good monitoring are what make maintenance cheap later.
  • Get maintenance scope, response times and account ownership in writing before launch.

What app maintenance covers

“Maintenance” is a broad word. In practice it is five kinds of work:

  • Bug fixes — problems users find on devices, networks or edge cases that testing did not cover.
  • Platform updates — keeping the app working and compliant with each new Android and iOS release, and with the latest store requirements.
  • Library and security updates — updating frameworks (such as Flutter or React Native), packages and server software, and patching security issues.
  • Monitoring and support — watching crash reports, server health and reviews, and answering user problems.
  • Small improvements — copy changes, minor UI fixes and small tweaks based on feedback.

New features are a separate thing. Adding a loyalty programme or a new user role is product development, and it is clearer for everyone when it is planned and priced on its own.

The yearly work you cannot skip

Some maintenance is your choice. This part is not:

  • New OS versions. Google and Apple release major Android and iOS updates every year. Permissions, notifications, background tasks and screen layouts can change, and apps must be tested and often adjusted.
  • Store requirements. Google Play requires apps to target a recent Android version to stay available to new users, and Apple regularly updates the SDK and Xcode versions needed to submit updates. Privacy forms and policies also change. Our guide to publishing on Google Play and the App Store explains these rules.
  • Third-party changes. Payment gateways, SMS and WhatsApp providers, maps and login services update or retire their APIs. When they do, your app must follow.
  • Security patches. Vulnerabilities are found in common libraries all the time. An app that holds customer data — and must handle it carefully under India’s DPDP Act — cannot leave these unpatched.
  • Developer accounts. The Apple Developer Program is an annual fee (US$99 per year at the time of writing), so keep it in the yearly budget.

Skip this work for a year or two and the cost does not disappear — it piles up. Catching up on several OS versions and outdated libraries at once is usually far more expensive than small regular updates.

Running costs: hosting and third-party services

These are bills from providers, not work by your team, and they mostly scale with usage:

Many of these have free tiers that cover a small app. Set up billing alerts on every account from day one, so a sudden spike — or a bug that sends thousands of OTPs — does not become a surprise invoice.

How to estimate your yearly budget

There is no single correct number, but you can get a realistic estimate in four steps:

  1. Start from the build cost. A common rule of thumb for the team’s maintenance work is 15–25% of the original build cost per year. Simple apps with few integrations sit at the lower end; apps with payments, real-time features and many integrations sit higher. See our guide to app development cost in India for what drives the build cost.
  2. Add running costs. Estimate monthly users and usage, then price hosting, messaging, maps and AI from each provider’s pricing page. Multiply by twelve and add a buffer for growth.
  3. Add fixed yearly fees. Developer accounts, domain, SSL if needed, and paid tools.
  4. Keep a separate feature budget. Real users will ask for things. Decide how much you want to spend on improvements — this is where the app actually gets better.

A quick example: if your first version cost ₹8 lakh to build, plan roughly ₹1.2–2 lakh a year for the team’s maintenance work, then add your running costs and feature budget on top. Treat this as a planning starting point, not a quote — your real figure depends on your app.

For most small and growing businesses, a modest retainer with the team that built the app is the simplest start: they already know the code, so fixes are faster. If you are working with a team abroad, our guide on hiring an app development team in India covers time zones and contracts.

How to keep maintenance costs down

  • Build a focused first version. Every feature you ship is a feature you maintain. An MVP keeps the surface area small.
  • Choose mainstream technology. Popular frameworks and well-supported services get updates and fixes quickly; obscure ones leave you stuck.
  • Insist on automated tests and clean code. They make every future change cheaper and safer.
  • Set up crash reporting and monitoring before launch, so problems are found by alerts, not by angry reviews.
  • Update little and often. Small monthly or quarterly updates cost less than one painful catch-up every two years.
  • Document the setup. Server access, build steps, accounts and keys should be written down so anyone can pick up the work.

What to put in a maintenance agreement

Before launch, agree in writing on:

  1. Scope — what is included (fixes, OS updates, security, monitoring) and what counts as new work.
  2. Response times — how fast critical issues (app down, payments failing) and normal issues are handled.
  3. Hours and pricing — monthly hours, the rate for extra hours and how the fee is reviewed.
  4. Ownership — the source code, store accounts, cloud accounts and domain stay in your company’s name.
  5. Handover — documentation and access so you can move to another team if you ever need to.

Common questions

How much does app maintenance cost per year in India?

It depends on the app’s size, users and integrations. A common planning rule of thumb is 15–25% of the original build cost per year for the team’s work, plus hosting and third-party services, which are billed separately and grow with usage.

Can I stop maintaining my app after launch?

Not safely. Both app stores require apps to keep up with new Android and iOS versions and policy changes. An app that is not updated can stop working properly on new phones, become unavailable to new users or be removed from the store.

What is included in an app maintenance contract?

Usually bug fixes, OS and library updates, security patches, server monitoring, store compliance and a fixed number of hours for small changes. New features are normally quoted separately. Always get the scope in writing.

Is hosting part of app maintenance cost?

Hosting, databases, SMS, email, maps and AI APIs are running costs, usually billed by the providers based on usage. Keep them in your budget, but track them separately from the team’s maintenance fee.

The bottom line

Launch is the start of an app’s life, not the end of the spending. Budget for two things every year — the team’s maintenance work and the running costs — and keep a separate budget for new features. Build lean, update often and keep your accounts in your own name, and maintenance stays a predictable line item instead of a surprise.
