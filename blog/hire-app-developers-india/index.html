Hiring an app development team in India: a guide for US startups

By the StaneAI team · 27 November 2026

India has one of the largest pools of software engineers in the world, and many US founders build their first product with a team here. Done well, it gives you senior-level work at a sustainable cost. Done badly, it gives you missed deadlines and code you cannot maintain. The difference is rarely the country — it is how you choose and work with the team. This guide covers what to check.

Key takeaways

  • Choose a partner for clear communication and product thinking, not only the lowest rate.
  • Agree on a few hours of daily overlap with your time zone and a fixed weekly demo.
  • Your contract must assign all IP and code to your company, with source code in your own repositories.
  • Start with a paid discovery phase or a small first milestone before committing to a large build.
  • Own every account yourself: code repository, cloud, app stores and domains.

Why US startups build with Indian teams

  • Talent depth. Experienced engineers in mobile (Flutter, React Native, native Android and iOS), web, cloud and AI.
  • Cost. You can usually afford a complete team — design, development, QA and project management — for what a small in-house US team would cost.
  • English-first work. Most product teams work in English day to day, with documentation, tickets and calls in English.
  • Follow-the-sun progress. With good hand-offs, work moves forward while your US team sleeps.

Engagement models

  • Fixed scope, fixed price. Good for a well-defined MVP. You need a clear specification, and changes go through a change request. Works best after a short discovery phase.
  • Time and materials. You pay for the hours or days used. Flexible when the product is still evolving; needs good tracking and weekly reviews.
  • Dedicated team. A team working only on your product each month, managed day to day by you or by the partner. Suits ongoing development after launch.

Many startups combine them: a fixed-price discovery and MVP, then a dedicated team for ongoing releases.

Time zones and communication

India Standard Time is UTC+5:30 and does not change for daylight saving. That puts India 9½–10½ hours ahead of US Eastern time and 12½–13½ hours ahead of Pacific time, depending on the season. In practice:

  • agree a 2–3 hour daily overlap — for example the Indian team’s evening and your morning;
  • keep a short async update every day in writing: what was done, what is next, what is blocked;
  • hold a weekly demo of working software, not just a status report;
  • use shared tools you can see — a ticket board, the code repository and a staging build you can open on your phone.

How to choose the right team

  1. Look at shipped work. Live apps in the stores and products you can try, not only design shots.
  2. Talk to the people who will build it. Meet the lead developer and designer, not only the salesperson.
  3. Test their questions. A good team asks about your users, business model and priorities, and pushes back on unnecessary features.
  4. Ask how they test and release. Code review, automated tests, QA on real devices, crash monitoring, staged releases.
  5. Check references from clients in similar time zones if possible.
  6. Start small. A paid discovery sprint or first milestone shows you how they communicate before you commit.

Contracts, IP and data

  • IP assignment. The contract should assign all intellectual property — code, designs, documentation — to your company on payment, with the developer waiving claims to it.
  • Source code access from day one. Code lives in a repository your company owns (GitHub, GitLab or Bitbucket), with the team invited as collaborators.
  • Accounts in your name. Cloud hosting, app store developer accounts, domains, analytics and third-party APIs should all be registered to your company.
  • Confidentiality. An NDA, and clear rules on who can access production data.
  • Data protection. If you handle US customer data, agree on where it is stored and who can access it. If the team processes data in India, India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act may also apply.
  • Governing law and exit terms. Agree which law applies, how disputes are resolved, and how hand-over works if you part ways.

Payments and pricing

  • Most Indian companies can invoice in US dollars and receive international wire transfers or payments through established platforms.
  • Tie payments to milestones you can see — a working build, a store release — rather than calendar dates alone.
  • Compare quotes on what is included: design, QA, project management, store publishing, documentation and post-launch support. A low hourly rate without QA often costs more in the end.
  • Budget for maintenance after launch: OS updates, fixes and small improvements.

Red flags

  • A quote within hours, without questions about your users or scope.
  • Promises to build everything, fast, for the lowest price.
  • Reluctance to put code in your repository or to publish under your accounts.
  • No demo for weeks, only status updates.
  • The person you met in sales disappears once the project starts.
  • No written test or release process.

The first 90 days

  1. Weeks 1–2: discovery. Users, core journey, scope, technology choice (for example Flutter or React Native), design direction and a release plan.
  2. Weeks 3–10: build in short sprints. A demo every week, a testable build on your phone every sprint.
  3. Weeks 10–12: test and launch. Beta testers, fixes, store submission — see our app publishing guide.

Common questions

Is it safe to outsource app development to India?

Yes, when the contract assigns all IP to your company, the code lives in your own repository from day one, and cloud, store and domain accounts are registered in your name. Those steps protect you with any partner, anywhere.

How do US startups manage the time difference with India?

Most teams agree two to three hours of daily overlap, post short written updates every day and hold a weekly demo. India is about 9.5 to 10.5 hours ahead of US Eastern time, depending on daylight saving.

Should I choose a freelancer or an agency in India?

A freelancer can suit a small, well-defined task. For a full product you usually need design, development, QA and project management together, which an agency or dedicated team provides with less risk if one person becomes unavailable.

Can an Indian team publish my app under my company?

Yes. You create the Google Play and Apple developer accounts in your company’s name and invite the team with the right roles. The app, its reviews and its revenue stay yours.

The bottom line

Building with a team in India works best when you treat it as a partnership with clear rules: overlap hours, weekly demos, code and accounts in your name, and a small first milestone before a big commitment. Choose for communication and product sense, and the cost advantage becomes a real advantage.
