Mobile app development in India: the complete guide for businesses

By the StaneAI team · 27 November 2026 · Long read

Building an app is one of the biggest product decisions a business makes. This guide brings together everything we explain to founders and business owners before a project starts — from whether you need an app at all, to choosing a technology, budgeting, picking a partner and getting through store review.

Key takeaways

  • Start from one clear problem and one type of user. A focused first version (MVP) launches sooner and costs less.
  • For most business apps in India, a cross-platform build (Flutter or React Native) that ships to both Android and iOS is the sensible default.
  • Cost and timeline depend mainly on scope: number of user types, integrations, design polish and the admin side.
  • Make sure the source code, store accounts, domain and cloud accounts are in your company’s name.
  • Plan for privacy, payments and store rules from day one, and budget for maintenance after launch.

Start with the problem, not the app

Most failed apps fail before a line of code is written: they solve a problem nobody has, or solve it in a way people will not change their habits for. Before you talk about screens and features, write down:

  • The problem, in one sentence, from your user’s point of view.
  • Who has it — be specific: “clinic receptionists in tier-2 cities”, not “healthcare”.
  • How they solve it today — phone calls, WhatsApp, paper, spreadsheets, a competitor’s app. Your app must be clearly better than this.
  • What success looks like — bookings per week, orders, time saved, repeat users.

Then test cheaply. Talk to ten potential users. Show them a simple clickable design. If they are not excited by the prototype, they will not be excited by the finished app.

Do you need an app? App vs website vs PWA

Not every idea needs an app from the store. Three options are worth comparing:

OptionBest forTrade-offs
Native or cross-platform appProducts people use often; notifications; camera, location, offline use; a strong presence on the phone’s home screenHigher build cost; store review; users must install it
Mobile-friendly websiteContent, information, occasional use, discovery through Google searchLimited access to device features; no home-screen habit
Progressive web app (PWA)Simple tools that should feel app-like without a store installPush notifications and device features are more limited, especially on iPhone

A common pattern is a fast website for marketing and search, plus an app for customers who use the service regularly. Both can share the same backend. Our guide website or mobile app: which first? has a six-question checklist to decide.

Android, iOS or both

In India, Android is used by most smartphone owners, across every price range. iPhone users are fewer but are often concentrated in higher-spending segments and are common in many export and premium markets. In practice:

  • Mass-market consumer apps in India usually start with Android or launch on both.
  • Premium, B2B or international products often need iOS from day one.
  • Internal business apps should follow the phones your team actually uses.

Our detailed guide on Android vs iOS: which platform to build first walks through the decision.

Native or cross-platform

You can build each platform separately (native) or build once and ship to both (cross-platform).

ApproachLanguagesStrengthsWatch out for
FlutterDartOne codebase, consistent custom design, smooth animationSlightly larger app size; smaller hiring pool than JavaScript
React NativeJavaScript / TypeScriptOne codebase, native controls, large talent pool, shares skills with React websitesQuality depends heavily on the team’s experience
NativeKotlin (Android), Swift (iOS)Best access to the newest platform features and hardwareTwo codebases, so more time and cost

For most business apps — bookings, ordering, payments, dashboards, chat — cross-platform is the right default. Choose native when the app depends on advanced hardware, heavy graphics or brand-new platform features. See Flutter vs React Native for a deeper comparison.

Behind every app is a backend: the server, database and APIs that store users, orders and content, plus an admin panel for your team. Budget and plan for these as seriously as the app itself.

Scope the first version (MVP)

Write down every feature you want, then sort them into “must have for the core journey”, “should have” and “later”. Version one gets only the first bucket — built well. Things that can be done manually behind the scenes at first, should be. Our guide What is an MVP? explains how to make these cuts with a worked example.

The phases of an app project

  1. Discovery — goals, users, core journeys, integrations, risks and a written scope.
  2. UX and UI design — user flows, wireframes, then the visual design and a clickable prototype.
  3. Architecture — choosing the stack, data model, APIs and hosting.
  4. Development — in short cycles (sprints), with a working build you can try every week or two.
  5. Testing — on real, low-end and high-end devices, on slow networks, and with real users.
  6. Launch — store listings, review, analytics and crash reporting switched on.
  7. Improve — read feedback and data, fix, and release regular updates.

Cost and timeline

There is no honest single price for “an app”. The main drivers are:

  • the number of user types (for example customer, vendor, delivery partner, admin);
  • the number and complexity of integrations — payments, maps, SMS/OTP, WhatsApp, CRMs, ERPs;
  • design polish and custom animation;
  • real-time features such as chat, live tracking or video;
  • platforms — one, two, plus a web app or not;
  • the admin panel and reporting your team needs.

A focused MVP is usually a matter of weeks to a few months; larger products with several user types take longer. We break both down in how much it costs to build an app in India and how long it takes to build an app.

Contracts, code and account ownership

This is where many businesses get hurt later. Agree in writing that:

  • your company owns the source code and design files, and receives them in a repository you control;
  • the Google Play Console and Apple Developer accounts are registered to your company;
  • the domain, cloud hosting, database, email/SMS and payment gateway accounts are in your name, with the developer given access;
  • there is a clear scope, milestone-based payment schedule, change-request process, warranty period and a confidentiality (NDA) clause.

Payments in Indian apps

Indian users expect UPI, cards, net banking and wallets. Payment gateways such as Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree, PhonePe and Paytm offer SDKs that handle this inside the app. Two rules to plan around:

  • Physical goods and real-world services (products, food, rides, appointments) can use a normal payment gateway.
  • Digital goods and subscriptions consumed in the app are generally subject to Google Play’s and Apple’s in-app billing rules. Check the current store policies before you design your pricing.

For recurring payments, look at UPI AutoPay and card mandates supported by your gateway.

Security, privacy and the DPDP Act

Collect only the personal data you need, explain why in a clear privacy policy, and protect it. India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) sets rules on consent, purpose limitation and data security for businesses handling personal data. Get legal advice on how it applies to your app. On the technical side, insist on:

  • encrypted connections (HTTPS) everywhere and no secrets stored in the app code;
  • secure login (OTP or passwordless done properly, rate limiting, session expiry);
  • role-based access in the admin panel;
  • backups, logging and a plan for what happens if something goes wrong.

Launching on Google Play and the App Store

  • Google Play needs a developer account, a store listing, screenshots, a privacy policy URL and the Data safety form. New personal developer accounts may need to run a closed test with testers before going public, so plan time for this.
  • Apple App Store needs an Apple Developer Program membership, App Store Connect listing, privacy “nutrition labels” and passes a human review that checks guidelines carefully.
  • Prepare a demo account for reviewers if your app needs a login, and make sure every feature mentioned in the listing works.
  • Turn on analytics and crash reporting before launch, not after.

Store fees and policies change; check the current requirements on the Google Play Console and Apple Developer websites. For the full step-by-step, see how to publish your app on Google Play and the App Store.

After launch: growth and maintenance

Launch is the start. Plan a budget and a team for:

  • Maintenance — new Android and iOS versions every year, library updates, security patches and store policy changes. See what to budget for app maintenance.
  • Improvements — features from the “should have” and “later” lists that real usage proves are needed.
  • Growth — store listing optimisation (title, screenshots, ratings), referral loops, onboarding improvements and push notifications that help rather than annoy.
  • Support — a way for users to reach you, and someone who reads reviews.

Many products also add AI after launch — for example a support chatbot or smart search. Our guide to AI for business covers when that makes sense.

A one-page checklist

  1. Problem, user and success metric written down
  2. App vs website decision made
  3. Platforms and technology chosen, with reasons
  4. MVP scope agreed: must-have features only
  5. Integrations listed (payments, OTP, maps, WhatsApp, CRM)
  6. Partner chosen after seeing real shipped apps
  7. Contract covers code ownership, accounts, milestones and warranty
  8. Privacy policy and data protection plan ready
  9. Analytics and crash reporting set up
  10. Post-launch budget for maintenance and improvements

Common questions

How much does it cost to build an app in India?

It depends on scope: user types, integrations, design and the admin side. A focused MVP costs a fraction of a full product. Ask for an estimate broken down by feature so you can decide what to cut.

How long does it take?

A focused MVP usually takes weeks to a few months including design, testing and store review. Larger products take longer and are best delivered in phases.

Should I build for Android or iOS first?

For mass-market Indian consumers, Android first or both together with a cross-platform framework. For premium, B2B or international audiences, include iOS early.

Who owns the app after it is built?

You should — make it explicit in the contract and make sure code, store accounts and cloud accounts are in your company’s name.

Can I add AI features later?

Yes. A clean backend makes it straightforward to add AI chat, smart search, document reading or automation once the core app works.
