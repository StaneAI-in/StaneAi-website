How to choose an AI development company: a practical checklist

By the StaneAI team · 21 November 2026

Many companies now offer “AI development”, and from the outside they can look very similar. Yet the right partner can turn an idea into a reliable tool your team uses every day, while the wrong one can leave you with an impressive demo that never works on real data. This practical checklist will help you compare AI development companies and ask the questions that reveal how they actually work.

Before you talk to anyone: define the problem

The best conversations with an AI company start with a business problem, not a technology. “We want to use AI” is hard to evaluate. “Our team spends hours every day reading invoices and typing the details into our accounting software” is something a good partner can assess, estimate and measure.

Write down:

  • The task or process you want to improve, and who does it today
  • Roughly how often it happens and how long it takes
  • What data is involved — documents, emails, chats, databases
  • What “success” would look like in practical terms

This one page will make every proposal you receive easier to compare.

The checklist

1. Do they ask more questions than they answer?

A good AI partner wants to understand your process, your data and your users before proposing a solution. Be cautious of anyone who promises a specific result in the first call without seeing your data. Thoughtful questions are a better sign than confident promises.

2. Will they tell you when AI isn’t the answer?

Some problems are better solved with simple rules, a better form or standard automation. An honest company will say so, even if it means a smaller project. Ask directly: “Is there a simpler way to solve this without AI?” The answer tells you a lot.

3. Can they show relevant, working examples?

Ask to see something they have built that is similar to your problem — a live product, a demo using realistic data, or a detailed walkthrough. Ask what went wrong along the way and how they fixed it. Teams with real experience talk comfortably about limitations and lessons learned.

4. Do they understand software, not just models?

The AI model is only one part of a useful system. The rest is ordinary but essential software work: user interfaces, integrations with your existing tools, databases, security, error handling, monitoring and deployment. Check that the team can build and support the whole product, not just a prototype in a notebook.

5. How will they handle your data?

Your data may include customer details, financial records or confidential documents. Ask:

  • Where will data be stored and processed?
  • Which third-party AI services will see it, and under what terms?
  • Who on their team will have access?
  • How will they comply with applicable law, such as India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023?
  • Will they sign a confidentiality agreement?

6. How will they measure accuracy?

AI systems make mistakes. The question is how often, and what happens when they do. A good partner will propose testing against real examples from your business, agree on acceptable accuracy for your use case, and design a review step — a human check — where errors would be costly. If a company says its AI “never makes mistakes”, treat that as a warning sign.

7. Do they suggest starting small?

A focused pilot or proof of concept on one well-defined task is the safest way to start. It shows whether the approach works on your data before you commit a large budget. Look for a partner who proposes clear phases with a decision point after each one.

8. Who owns what at the end?

Clarify ownership early. Will you own the source code, the prompts and configuration, the trained models (if any) and the data? Can you move the system to another provider later? Which accounts — cloud hosting, AI services, app stores — will be in your company’s name?

9. What are the running costs?

Unlike traditional software, many AI systems have usage-based costs: you pay the AI provider for each request or volume of text processed. Ask for an estimate of monthly running costs at your expected usage, and how those costs change if usage grows. Include hosting, monitoring and maintenance too.

10. How will they communicate during the project?

Ask how often you will see working progress, who your day-to-day contact will be, and how changes in scope are handled. Regular demos of working software are far more reliable than status reports.

11. What happens after launch?

AI systems need ongoing attention: AI providers update their models, your data and processes change, and real users find edge cases no one predicted. Ask what support, monitoring and improvement the company offers after launch, and what it costs.

Red flags to watch for

  • Guaranteed results or accuracy figures before seeing your data
  • Vague answers about data handling and third-party services
  • No clear answer on code and data ownership
  • A large fixed price for an unclear scope, with no pilot phase
  • Heavy use of buzzwords with little discussion of your actual process
  • No plan for testing, human review or maintenance

Questions to copy into your first call

  1. Based on what you have heard, would you use AI for this — and why?
  2. Can you show us something similar you have built?
  3. What would a small first phase look like, and what would we learn from it?
  4. How will you test accuracy, and what happens when the system is wrong?
  5. Where will our data go, and who will have access to it?
  6. What will we own at the end of the project?
  7. What are the expected monthly running costs?
  8. What does support look like after launch?

The bottom line

Choosing an AI development company is less about who uses the newest model and more about who understands your problem, handles your data responsibly, tests honestly and builds software that keeps working after the demo. Start with a clear problem, use this checklist to compare partners, and begin with a focused pilot.
